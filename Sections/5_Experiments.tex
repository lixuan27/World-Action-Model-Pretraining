\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\Paragraph{Benchmarks.}
Three closed-loop suites carry the study, and each one gets its own result table
so that no comparison spans two benchmarks. LIBERO
\citep{liu2023libero} is a single-arm Franka tabletop benchmark whose forty tasks
divide into four families that separate spatial reasoning, object identity, goal
specification, and long-horizon composition; it measures precision inside the
training distribution. LIBERO-Plus \citep{liberoplus} rescores the same tasks
under seven independent perturbation axes, namely camera placement, robot initial
state, language phrasing, lighting, background, sensor noise, and object layout;
it measures perturbation robustness on one embodiment. RoboTwin 2.0
\citep{chen2025robotwin2} is a bimanual benchmark of fifty tasks on the
Aloha-Agilex platform with a clean split, a domain-randomised split that alters
table height, clutter, background, and lighting, and two further arm types,
ARX-X5 and Piper, that appear only at evaluation time; it measures
in-distribution precision, layout generalisation, and zero-shot embodiment
transfer. None of the three enters pretraining.

\Paragraph{Model, corpus, and budget.}
Every arm uses the world stream and the action stream of \sref{sec:streams} at
their stated sizes. The coupling axis changes one attention mask and one
stop-gradient, so its arms are parameter-matched by construction; the single
arm with a different parameter count is the single-stream structural control,
which concatenates the action tokens into the video sequence and removes the
second stream. Pretraining runs use the 6\,451 hour corpus of
\tref{tab:corpus} and share one step budget across recipes, so a recipe
comparison separates corpus composition from corpus size. Fine-tuning runs
consume twenty thousand optimiser steps at sixteen windows per device. Compute is
a per-user allocation of sixteen accelerators with eighty gigabytes each, with
short jobs permitted to overflow to forty-eight under a preemptible queue.

\Paragraph{Protocol, floors, and statistics.}
Closed-loop scores come from the official client of each benchmark driven
through the policy server of \sref{sec:procedure}, with fifty trials per task, a
fixed initial-state seed, and the evaluation seed recorded in the result file.
Main tables report the mean over three training seeds. Every comparison between
two arms is step-matched, uses the same data manifest and the same seed set, and
is tested with a paired bootstrap over tasks at the ninety-five percent level; a
difference counts as established when the interval excludes zero. Each
representation measurement declares its degenerate floors in advance, and a
measurement that fails to clear its floors is reported as such. Every run writes
a metrics file and a manifest holding the resolved configuration, the commit, and
the data fingerprint, and the experiment ledger binds each number in this
section to one such file.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Performance}
\label{sec:closedloop}

Three tables carry the closed-loop study, one per benchmark, and all three share
one row set. Three representation settings appear, namely a passive world model
with a policy readout trained on top of it, the independent-objective arm, and
the bidirectional arm, and each appears without embodied pretraining and with
the full corpus. Fixing the mainline arm as the bidirectional coupling without
embodied pretraining is what makes the sweep of \sref{sec:sweeps} interpretable,
because every arm of that sweep then shares one set of starting weights; the
pretrained rows then show what the corpus adds on top.

Published policies supply the external comparison. They enter each table only
where a published number exists for that benchmark, and every one of them
received large-scale pretraining of its own, so the pretrained rows of this work
are the ones that compare with them directly. The pre-registered criterion for
the mainline arm is to reach the strongest published average on LIBERO, and the
criterion for the pretrained arm is to exceed it. The shifted suites carry no
parity criterion for the arm without embodied pretraining, because a model that
has seen no robot corpus is not expected to match a pretrained policy under
distribution shift.

\subsection{Controlled Sweeps}
\label{sec:sweeps}

\tref{tab:sweeps} sweeps one variable at a time and holds everything else fixed.
The first panel walks the coupling axis of \sref{sec:coupling} through its three
degrees of freedom without embodied pretraining, so parameters, data, optimiser,
and seeds are identical across its rows. Two hypotheses that a benchmark average
alone confounds separate here: the auxiliary hypothesis lets the action stream
read a world representation it cannot shape, and the joint hypothesis lets the
action objective shape that representation. The second panel varies the
pretraining corpus at a fixed step budget, from no pretraining through robot
sources alone, then human video with an empty action mask, then the same human
footage with consequence labels. The third panel holds the recipe fixed and
varies the corpus fraction, which separates composition from volume. The fourth
panel restricts the corpus to a subset whose action space is joint angles and
whose camera is fixed, which decouples the action from its visual effect and
therefore removes the signal the coupling is designed to exploit. The three
score columns are the LIBERO average, the LIBERO-Plus average, and the RoboTwin
randomised split, so each column is one benchmark. The final column reports the
action loss at the first fine-tuning step, which reads how much of the
downstream action mapping a configuration already carries.

\tref{tab:sweeps} reports the four sweeps, and panel (b) of \fref{fig:results}
plots the corpus ladder.

\subsection{What the Frozen Representation Encodes}
\label{sec:probes}

The world-clamped ray of \sref{sec:paths} turns a trained model into an inverse
dynamics model, and a linear probe on the hidden states of the world stream
measures the same property without running the sampler. Every arm is frozen, the
hidden state of each world layer is read, and one ridge regression per layer
predicts the future action chunk, the future consequence trajectory, and the
same consequence trajectory on a held-out embodiment. Probe data comes from the
LIBERO validation split, which carries consequence labels, and the held-out
embodiment is the RoboTwin Aloha-Agilex platform. Four floors bound the
measurement: a randomly initialised backbone of the same shape gives the score
attainable from architecture alone, a shuffled-label probe gives the score
attainable from the marginal statistics of the target, a copy-the-first-step
predictor gives the score attainable from temporal smoothness, and a
cross-embodiment mean predictor gives the score attainable without reading the
observation at all. The held-out column is the test of whether the consequence
representation is shared across embodiments rather than tied to one kinematic
chain.

\tref{tab:probes} reports the probes, and panel (a) of \fref{fig:results} shows
their depth profile.

\subsection{Whether the Generated Future Follows the Condition}
\label{sec:clamped}

Two measurements run on one trained model along the clamped rays of
\sref{sec:paths}, both on the LIBERO validation split. The first asks whether the generated future follows the action
rather than merely correlating with it. The action tokens are clamped to four
different chunks and the world stream is denoised to completion, and the
displacement of the manipulator keypoints in the generated future is compared
with the displacement the clamped chunk implies. The true chunk is the condition
of interest; a chunk from a different episode, a zero chunk, and a time-shuffled
chunk are the three floors, and a passive world model admits no action input at
all and marks the uncontrollable end of the scale. The second asks which level of
grounding produces the more controllable future, which the same rays answer by
clamping the action stream, the consequence stream, and both.

\tref{tab:clamped} reports both measurements, and panel (c) of

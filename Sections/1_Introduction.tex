\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large-scale video pretraining has become the standard way to give an embodied
agent prior knowledge about the physical world. A video generative model learns
the statistics of how scenes evolve, so it acquires object permanence, contact
events, and the temporal regularity of everyday manipulation from footage that
needs no action annotation. Robot learning takes that prior in two ways. One way
generates a future observation and recovers a control command from it with a
separate module. The other way attaches an action decoder to the pretrained
backbone and fine-tunes both on teleoperation data. Both ways treat the
pretraining stage as a model of observation dynamics, which is a model of what
usually happens next in the world.

Embodied interaction places a further requirement on that prior. An agent has to
know how its own actions participate in the evolution it observes, because
control requires selecting among the futures the agent can actually bring about.
A model of observation dynamics marginalises the causal variable it needs, since
the actions that produced the training footage are integrated out. The resulting
representation supports prediction of the likely future and leaves the mapping
from an intervention to its visual effect unconstrained. This is the gap the
paper addresses, and we refer to closing it as action grounding, meaning that the
pretrained representation encodes how actions constrain and produce changes in
the world rather than encoding the changes alone.

We formulate action grounding as a joint generative problem and call the
formulation Joint Action-World Modeling, abbreviated \sys{}. A single continuous
flow-matching process generates the future observation and the future action
chunk together, and the two token streams exchange information through one
shared attention space at every layer of the backbone. Two properties make this a
pretraining principle rather than an added prediction head. The two streams carry
independently drawn noise levels, so the training distribution covers a whole
plane of noise-level pairs and every monotone path through that plane is a valid
sampler of the trained model; one model therefore instantiates a policy, an
action-conditioned world model, and an inverse dynamics model as three
conditional slices of the same learned distribution. The exchange is
bidirectional and carries gradient in both directions, so the action objective
shapes the world representation as well as reading it. Those two properties turn coupling into a
single experimental axis with three degrees of freedom, namely cross-stream
visibility, gradient flow, and coupling depth, and the axis separates joint
modelling from an auxiliary action head under matched parameters, data, and
optimisation.

The formulation extends one step further, from the command an agent issues to the
change that command produces. A motor command specifies how the actuators move
and leaves the resulting change in the world implicit. The consequence of an
action, defined here as the displacement of a set of world keypoints over the
next few control steps together with their observability, specifies the change
directly in image coordinates. Consequence supervision therefore reaches data
that carries no action label, because egocentric human footage shows the effect
of a manipulation even when the underlying commands are unavailable. Consequence
also gives a probe of representation content that does not depend on the action
space of any single embodiment, so the same measurement applies across robots
with different kinematics. We treat consequence as a third generated stream
inside the same joint process, which keeps one formulation, one objective, and
one model tree.

This paper delivers the formulation, its implementation, the pretraining corpus
that mixes robot trajectories with egocentric human footage, and a
pre-registered measurement programme built around one question: whether
structurally coupling action generation to world generation changes measurable
properties of the world representation, and whether those changes explain
downstream control behaviour. The programme declares its floors, its criteria,
and the conditions under which the coupling stops paying, so a negative outcome
is recorded rather than absorbed. Published policies supply the external
comparison on each of the three benchmarks the study uses, and every value the
programme still has to measure is marked as a target in the text and in the
source. \sref{sec:joint} develops the
formulation and the measurement instruments it implies. \sref{sec:pretrain}
gives the realisation, the interfaces that let heterogeneous sources enter one
objective, and the pretraining procedure. \sref{sec:exp} states the programme
and its result tables.

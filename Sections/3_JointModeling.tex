\section{Joint Action-World Modeling}
\label{sec:joint}

\subsection{One Joint Distribution and Its Conditional Slices}
\label{sec:slices}

Write $o_0$ for the current observation, $\ell$ for the language instruction,
$o_{1:T}$ for the future observations, and $a_{1:H}$ for the future action chunk.
Three familiar model classes each learn a different distribution over these
quantities, and \tref{tab:slices} places them side by side. A passive world
model learns $p(o_{1:T}\mid o_0,\ell)$ and integrates the action out, so it
describes what usually follows the current observation. A policy learns
$p(a_{1:H}\mid o_0,\ell)$ and receives no explicit constraint from how the world
responds. An action-conditioned world model learns
$p(o_{1:T}\mid o_0,a_{1:H},\ell)$ and treats the action as an exogenous input
that it evaluates rather than a variable it models.

\sys{} learns the joint distribution
\begin{equation}
  p_\theta\!\left(o_{1:T},\, a_{1:H}\,\middle|\, o_0,\ell\right),
  \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
and the three classes above are conditional or marginal slices of
\eqref{eq:joint}. The paper studies what the joint objective changes about the
learned representation, and the slices become the instruments of that study
because each one is obtainable from a single trained network.

\subsection{Joint Flow Matching on the Action-World Noise Plane}
\label{sec:plane}

\Paragraph{Two streams, two noise levels.}
\fref{fig:framework} draws the construction. A frozen video autoencoder maps
the observation clip to a latent grid
$z_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{C\times T\times h\times w}$ whose first temporal slice is
the clean current observation. The action chunk $a_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{A\times D}$
lives in the shared slot layout of \sref{sec:interfaces}. Both quantities are
noised by the same rectified-flow interpolation
\citep{lipman2023flowmatching,liu2023rectifiedflow}, and each carries its own
noise level,
\begin{equation}
  z_{\tw} = (1-\tw)\,z_0 + \tw\,\epsilon_w,
  \qquad
  a_{\ta} = (1-\ta)\,a_0 + \ta\,\epsilon_a,
  \qquad
  \epsilon_w,\epsilon_a \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I).
  \label{eq:interp}
\end{equation}
Noise levels are drawn through the time warp
\begin{equation}
  f_\lambda(u) = \frac{\lambda u}{1+(\lambda-1)u},
  \qquad u\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1],
  \qquad \lambda = 5,
  \label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
which concentrates training on high-noise levels \citep{esser2024sd3}. One
network consumes both noisy streams together with the conditioning and returns
one velocity field per stream,
\begin{equation}
  \left(v_w, v_a\right)
  = f_\theta\!\left(z_{\tw},\, a_{\ta};\ \tw,\ \ta,\ o_0,\ \ell,\ s\right),
  \label{eq:net}
\end{equation}
where $s$ is the proprioceptive state at the observation step. Writing
$\|\cdot\|^2_{M}$ for the mean squared error restricted to the entries a boolean
mask $M$ selects, the objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) =
  \mathbb{E}\Big[\,
    \lambda_w\, \omega(\tw)\,\big\|v_w - (\epsilon_w - z_0)\big\|^2_{M_w}
    \;+\;
    \lambda_a\, \omega(\ta)\,\big\|v_a - (\epsilon_a - a_0)\big\|^2_{M_a}
  \Big].
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The world mask $M_w$ selects the future latent frames and drops the clean
current observation together with any padded frame. The action mask $M_a$ selects
the horizon steps and action dimensions the source actually measures, which is
the mechanism that lets one action head serve many embodiments and lets
action-free footage contribute to the world term alone. The level weight
$\omega$ is a bell profile centred at $\tw = \tfrac{1}{2}$, normalised to unit
mean under the warped sampling distribution, and shared by both streams.

\Paragraph{Independent levels make the sampler a free variable.}
The single decision that separates \eqref{eq:loss} from a diffusion policy with a
video loss attached is that $\tw$ and $\ta$ are drawn independently for every
training example.

\begin{proposition}[Plane coverage]
\label{prop:plane}
Let $\tw$ and $\ta$ be drawn independently through \eqref{eq:warp} for every
example. Then the joint law of the level pair has full support on
$[0,1]^2$, and the minimiser of \eqref{eq:loss} matches the conditional
expectations $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_w - z_0 \mid z_{\tw}, a_{\ta}]$ and
$\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_a - a_0 \mid z_{\tw}, a_{\ta}]$ at almost every point of
that square.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Independence makes the joint law the product of two marginals, each of which has
a positive density on $(0,1)$ because $f_\lambda$ is a diffeomorphism of $[0,1]$
for $\lambda>0$. Conditioned on a fixed level pair, \eqref{eq:loss} is a pair of
weighted least-squares regressions whose minimisers are the stated conditional
expectations. The level pair enters $f_\theta$ as an input and the losses at
distinct pairs are additive, so the pointwise minimisers are attained together.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:plane} has an immediate consequence. Every monotone path
from $(\tw,\ta)=(1,1)$ to $(0,0)$ lies inside the
support of the training distribution, so the choice of path belongs to
deployment. Tying the two
levels together during training would supervise the diagonal alone and would fix
the sampler in advance.

\Paragraph{Boundary rays.}
The two edges of the square carry the two conditional slices of
\sref{sec:slices} that matter most, and \eqref{eq:warp} places vanishing
probability on them. \sys{} therefore spends a small fraction $p_b$ of the
training mass on the edges: with probability $p_b/2$ the action level is pinned
to $\ta=0$ and the action term is removed from the loss, which trains the
action-conditioned world model explicitly, and with probability $p_b/2$ the world
level is pinned to $\tw=0$ and the world term is removed, which trains the
inverse dynamics model explicitly. A pinned stream is a condition rather than a
target, so its loss weight is zero. The change costs one scalar in the sampling
distribution and follows directly from \eqref{eq:joint}.

\subsection{Three Paths, Three Instruments}
\label{sec:paths}

Sampling walks a path $\{(\tw^{(k)},\ta^{(k)})\}_{k=0}^{K}$ that ends at
$(0,0)$, taking one Euler step per stream per iteration,
\begin{equation}
  x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} + \big(\tau^{(k+1)} - \tau^{(k)}\big)\, v^{(k)},
  \label{eq:euler}
\end{equation}
and re-pinning the clean observation frame after every step. Three paths matter,
and each one measures a different property of the trained model.

The \emph{joint path} follows \eqref{eq:euler} with both levels in lockstep and
is the control mode:
it samples an action chunk and the future it induces together, and the action
chunk drives the robot under receding-horizon execution.

The \emph{action-clamped ray} holds $\ta\equiv0$ with the action tokens fixed to
a supplied chunk and lets only the world level descend, which realises the
action-conditioned world model. Supplying the true chunk, a chunk from a
different episode, a zero chunk, and a time-shuffled chunk turns this ray into a
counterfactual test of whether the generated future follows the action.

The \emph{world-clamped ray} holds $\tw\equiv0$ with the future latent frames
fixed to their ground truth and lets only the action level descend, which
realises inverse dynamics. The quality of the recovered chunk measures how much
action information the world representation carries.

A fourth family of paths lets one stream lead the other by a fixed offset in the
progress variable, which turns the synchrony of the two streams into an ablation
rather than a design commitment.

\subsection{Coupling as One Experimental Axis}
\label{sec:coupling}

\Paragraph{One sequence, one mask.}
Both streams present their tokens to a single attention over the concatenation
\begin{equation}
  x = \big[\;\underbrace{z_{\tw}}_{\text{world}}\;\big|\;
      \underbrace{s}_{\text{condition}}\;\big|\;
      \underbrace{a_{\ta}}_{\text{action}}\;\big|\;
      \underbrace{c_{\ta}}_{\text{consequence}}\;\big],
  \label{eq:seq}
\end{equation}
and one boolean matrix $M$ decides which query reads which key. Three blocks of
$M$ stay fixed across every configuration. The clean observation tokens attend to
themselves alone, which keeps the conditioning prefix a self-contained
representation of the present and keeps its keys reusable across denoising
iterations. The action-side tokens attend freely among themselves, because a
chunk is a jointly denoised trajectory. The state token is a condition that the
noisy world tokens may always read, so the axis below varies only what the
streams read of each other's \emph{generated} content.

\Paragraph{Three degrees of freedom.}
The remaining blocks of $M$ form the experimental axis of the paper, and
\tref{tab:coupling} lists the settings with the competing hypothesis each one
realises. Cross-stream visibility takes four values. Setting \vnone{} keeps the
two streams independent and reduces \eqref{eq:loss} to two objectives that share
a data loader. Setting \vwa{} lets action queries read the world stream, which
is the world-model-plus-readout hypothesis. Setting \vaw{} lets noisy world
queries read the action tokens, which is the action-conditioned hypothesis.
Setting \vbi{} enables both directions and is \sys{}. Gradient flow is the second
degree of freedom: detaching the world keys and values that the action stream
reads leaves the action stream able to read the world representation while
removing its ability to shape that representation, which is exactly an auxiliary
action head attached to a trunk the video objective owns. Coupling depth is the
third degree of freedom and selects the subset of backbone layers at which the
exchange happens. \fref{fig:masks} draws the visibility settings, and the
combination of \vwa{} with detached keys names the auxiliary baseline that the
experiments have to separate from \vbi{}.

\subsection{From Command to Consequence}
\label{sec:consequence}

A motor command specifies actuator motion and leaves the resulting change in the
world implicit. The consequence of an action specifies that change directly. We
define a consequence label for a window as the displacement of $K$ world
keypoint slots over the next $H_c$ control steps together with their
observability,
\begin{equation}
  c_{0}[\tau,k] = \Big(\,g\cdot\big(u_k(\tau) - u_k(0)\big),\ \ 2\nu_k(\tau)-1\,\Big)
  \in \mathbb{R}^3,
  \qquad \tau = 1,\dots,H_c,
  \label{eq:consequence}
\end{equation}
where $u_k(\tau)$ is the image position of slot $k$ in camera-normalised
coordinates, $u_k(0)$ is its anchor at the observation step, $\nu_k \in [0,1]$ is
its observability, and $g$ scales the displacement so the flow target has unit
order of magnitude. Slots that a source cannot supply are masked out of the loss.
Actuator slots are anchored at their tracked position at the observation step,
and scene probe slots are anchored at fixed query locations on a grid, which
gives coverage of the background as well as the manipulator.

Three properties make consequence the natural next step of \eqref{eq:joint}
rather than a separate method. Consequence labels need no action annotation,
because a tracker recovers them from video alone
\citep{doersch2023tapir,karaev2024cotracker}, so egocentric human footage can
ground the action side of the model. Consequence is defined in image
coordinates, so the same slot table applies to platforms with different
kinematics and the resulting probe transfers across embodiments. Consequence is
the quantity the grounding mechanism is supposed to be about: if coupling helps
because the action constrains how the world changes, then supervising the change
itself should provide grounding of the same kind.

\sys{} therefore admits consequence as a third generated stream that shares the
action noise level, which extends \eqref{eq:joint} to
\begin{equation}
  p_\theta\!\left(o_{1:T},\, a_{1:H},\, c_{1:H_c}\,\middle|\, o_0,\ell\right),
  \label{eq:joint3}
\end{equation}
adds one term $\lambda_c\,\omega(\ta)\|v_c-(\epsilon_c-c_0)\|^2_{M_c}$ to
\eqref{eq:loss}, and places the consequence tokens in the action-side block of
\eqref{eq:seq} so they inherit the visibility setting under study. The clamped
rays of \sref{sec:paths} extend accordingly: clamping the consequence stream
in place of the action stream gives a consequence-conditioned world model, and
comparing the two clamps on one trained network measures which level of grounding
gives the more controllable future.
